Context

There was not an established content base to inherit.

When I started working on Learniverse, the platform did not have a meaningful blog presence. That is a different starting point from an established site where you can look at years of rankings, traffic, and existing pages before deciding what to do next.

Here, the first job was simply to build enough useful content for the site to start becoming discoverable.

What I did

I helped create the content foundation rather than optimise an existing one.

That meant producing the organic content itself and helping the site move from almost no blog footprint toward a body of pages that search engines and readers could actually discover.

I am keeping the description broad because I no longer have the old briefs, calendars, or detailed planning documents needed to reconstruct the exact workflow.

What building from zero meant

Early progress looked different because there was no baseline yet.

Before a content program can be heavily performance-led, there has to be enough content and traffic to learn from. The first useful milestone was creating a real organic footprint.

    What I cannot prove now

    I do not have the old files needed to rebuild the exact strategy retrospectively.

    I no longer have the original briefs, calendars, analytics exports, or detailed strategy records. Rather than inventing a polished retrospective framework after the fact, I am keeping this case smaller and more honest.

    What I can state confidently: Learniverse started without a meaningful blog presence, I built the content from scratch, and the platform was receiving roughly 2,500 monthly organic visits by the time I stopped working with it.

    What I took from it

    Building the first content base and scaling an existing one are different jobs.

    DigitalAPI shows what happens when there is an existing site and enough data to review performance. Learniverse was much closer to creating the foundation in the first place. I have worked on both sides of that problem.
