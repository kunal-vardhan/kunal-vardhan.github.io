Case study 01 · API platform

From roughly 30 to 3,000+ monthly organic visits in under five months.

A content-strategy case study on topical authority, intent mapping, pillar pages, technical content, internal linking, and performance-led refreshes.

Context

Starting from almost no organic baseline.

The API platform was sitting at roughly 30 monthly organic visits. The opportunity was not to chase isolated keywords one by one, but to create enough connected coverage around the product’s core use cases that search engines and buyers could both understand what the platform was relevant for.

Core problem: low organic visibility, fragmented topic coverage, and a need for a repeatable content system rather than one-off publishing.

Strategic direction

Build topical authority around use cases and intent.

The strategy centered on a cluster model. Topics were mapped to specific search-intent stages, pillar pages were used to organize the larger subject areas, and internal linking connected supporting content back to those core pages.

What shaped the roadmap

  • Keyword and competitor gaps around core API use cases.
  • Search intent across educational, comparison, problem-solving, and commercial topics.
  • Topic clusters and pillar pages instead of disconnected article ideas.
  • Quarterly prioritization based on search demand and business relevance.

Execution

Turn the strategy into a production and refresh loop.

Execution covered keyword research, cluster planning, brief creation, production, internal linking, and performance review. The content itself spanned technical education, API management, software comparisons, pricing, frameworks, marketplaces, and troubleshooting topics.

Underperforming content was not treated as finished. Pages were reviewed and refreshed to maintain ranking momentum as the cluster grew.

Results

Organic traffic grew from roughly 30 to 3,000+ monthly visits in under five months.

  • 30 → 3,000+monthly organic visits
  • < 5 monthstimeframe for the reported growth
  • Cluster-ledpillar pages + supporting content + internal linking
  • Ongoingrefreshes used to protect ranking momentum

What this shows

Content strategy works better as a system than as a calendar.

This project is the clearest example of how I prefer to work: start with a business-relevant topic universe, map it to search intent, build connected coverage, make execution repeatable, and use performance data to decide what happens next.
