Case study 01 · DigitalAPI

From 29 to 3,842 monthly Organic Search sessions.

A content system built around connected API topics, search intent, repeatable production, internal links, and performance review rather than isolated keyword assignments.

Context

The opportunity was bigger than publishing more API articles.

DigitalAPI was operating in a technical category where the same buyer can search from very different starting points. One person may be learning what API management is. Another may be troubleshooting a rate-limit problem. Someone closer to a buying decision may be comparing tools, pricing, frameworks, or implementation choices.

That meant the content could not work as a queue of unrelated keywords. It needed enough structure for individual pages to answer their own search question while still strengthening a larger body of API-management coverage.

My role

I worked across the system, not only the draft.

The writing mattered, but it sat inside a broader operating loop. I researched search demand and competing pages, grouped related opportunities, worked from briefs, produced content, connected relevant pages through internal links, and used performance signals to decide what deserved another pass.

How the work moved from strategy to performance

A simplified view of the operating loop used for this kind of program.

1 · UnderstandProduct, audience, use cases, search language and business relevance
2 · StructurePillars, clusters, intent, briefs and internal-link paths
3 · ExecuteResearch, writing, editing, publishing and contextual product connection
4 · ImproveReview performance, refresh weak pages and expand themes that earn traction

Strategic problem

Technical search demand was fragmented across education, troubleshooting and buying intent.

If every query had been treated as a standalone article, the site could have accumulated content without building a coherent topic footprint. The better approach was to ask what role each page played for the reader and how it connected to the wider API-management subject.

TOFU · Understand

Category and concept education

Explain what a concept is, why it matters, and where it fits in an API workflow.

MOFU · Solve

Use cases and troubleshooting

Help readers diagnose practical problems such as rate limits, governance or framework decisions.

BOFU · Evaluate

Cost, tools and comparisons

Answer commercial questions that appear when a buyer is evaluating approaches or vendors.

Content system

The important part was how the topics connected.

The published work covered API management, cost, frameworks, marketplaces, tools, comparisons and troubleshooting. Those subjects naturally support different intents, so the strategy was to make them reinforce one another rather than sit as isolated URLs.

What shaped prioritisation

  • Search intent: what the searcher is actually trying to understand, fix, compare, or buy.
  • Business relevance: how naturally the topic connects to the product and target audience.
  • Topic coverage: whether the site already had enough supporting depth around the subject.
  • Internal-link opportunity: which pages should guide readers toward deeper education or a relevant product path.
  • Existing performance: whether an older page was worth improving before commissioning another new one.

Execution

Each piece still had to work on its own.

A cluster does not rescue a weak article. Every page still needed research, a clear answer, enough technical context, sensible structure, and a reason for the reader to continue. The production loop therefore combined the strategic map with page-level execution.

Page-level execution

ResearchSERP, competing pages, product context, terminology and sources
BriefIntent, angle, structure, questions to answer and internal-link targets
ProduceDraft, fact-check, edit and make the technical explanation readable
ReviewMeasure the page and decide whether to refresh, strengthen or expand

What this shows

The system mattered because every new page had somewhere to fit.

The most useful lesson from this work is not “publish more.” It is that research, topic architecture, production, internal linking and performance review work better when they are treated as one connected content operation.

That is also how I prefer to approach content strategy now: start with the business and buyer, define what deserves to exist, make the production system repeatable, then let performance influence the next cycle.
