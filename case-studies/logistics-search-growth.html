FarEye · Logistics SaaS · Case File No. 02

Writing for logistics readers who were searching from very different starting points.

The work moved between operational education and software evaluation across last-mile delivery, routing, tracking, direct-store delivery, returns, and logistics-management systems.

Ahrefs page-level evidence. Useful for visibility, not treated as first-party traffic.

Context

Logistics buyers do not search only for “logistics software.”

A logistics operator may start with a workflow question: how direct-store delivery works, how carrier tracking fits into the last mile, why route planning matters, or how delivery visibility affects operations. Someone further along may search directly for logistics-management or routing software.

That gave the content room to work across the journey. Some pages needed to teach. Some needed to help solve an operational problem. Others needed to help a reader compare software.

What I owned

The first job was understanding the operation well enough to explain it.

Logistics terminology can sound simple from the outside, but the workflow underneath it usually is not. I had to understand how the process works, where it breaks, why the problem matters, and when software becomes relevant.

01

The workflow

Who does what, where handoffs happen, and what the normal process looks like.

02

The problem

The delay, cost, visibility gap, exception, or decision the reader is dealing with.

03

The search

The language the reader uses and the answer the SERP suggests they expect.

04

The product fit

Where software genuinely belongs in the explanation without forcing it into the page.

Software evaluationHigher-intent pages for readers comparing logistics tools, categories, capabilities, and options.
Returns and exceptionsProblem-led content around delivery exceptions, returns, and post-purchase operations.

How the topics differed

A direct-store-delivery explainer and a software page are not doing the same job.

How I approached them

I tried to find the practical question underneath the keyword.

That question decided the structure, the amount of background needed, the examples, the terminology, and how much product context belonged on the page.

  • Explain the operation before assuming the reader understands the terminology.
  • Show what goes wrong and why the problem matters in practice.
  • Use evaluation criteria when the reader is clearly comparing software.
  • Connect to the product only where the connection makes sense.

What I took from it

Complex-industry content gets easier to write when the workflow is clear first.

The useful part was learning enough about the operation to understand what the reader was actually trying to do. Once that was clear, the writing, search intent, and product connection became much easier to handle.
