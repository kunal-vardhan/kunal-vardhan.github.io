Helping logistics content rank for the questions buyers were already searching for.

Context

Logistics buyers do not search only for “logistics software.”

A logistics operator may start with a workflow problem: how direct-store delivery works, how carrier tracking fits into the last mile, why a route plan breaks, or how delivery exceptions affect the customer experience. A buyer further along may search directly for routing or logistics-management software.

That creates a content opportunity across the journey. Educational pages can build understanding and topical depth, while evaluation pages can meet a reader closer to a software decision.

My role

My first job was to understand the operation well enough to explain it.

Logistics terminology can sound simple from the outside, but the practical workflows are not. The research had to establish how the process actually works, what failure looks like, what the reader is trying to improve, and where software becomes relevant.

The research-to-content bridge

The product should enter after the operational problem is clear, not before.

WorkflowUnderstand the real delivery, tracking, routing or distribution process
ProblemIdentify the friction, cost, delay, visibility gap or evaluation need
Search intentMatch the language and question the reader is actually using
Product fitConnect software only where it genuinely helps solve the problem

Buyer journey coverage

The work covered more than one stage of intent.

TOFU · Learn

Operational education

Direct-store delivery, logistics concepts, distribution processes and the mechanics behind the workflow.

MOFU · Solve

Visibility and problem solving

Last-mile carrier tracking, delivery visibility, route planning and the operational consequences of poor execution.

BOFU · Evaluate

Software selection

Routing software, logistics-management software and other pages where the reader is actively comparing technology.

That mix matters because a software company should not appear only when someone types a vendor-style query. Stronger coverage helps the brand meet the buyer earlier, teach the category, and still have useful pages ready when commercial intent appears.

How I approached it

Make the workflow understandable, then make the page useful for the decision.

For each topic I looked for the practical question underneath the keyword. That shaped the structure, examples, terminology and amount of product context the page needed.

How the content moved from broad to commercial

EducateExplain the process, actors and terminology without assuming expertise
DiagnoseShow what goes wrong and why the problem matters operationally
EvaluateIntroduce criteria for comparing approaches or software

Representative themes

  • Direct-store delivery and distribution processes.
  • Last-mile carrier tracking and delivery visibility.
  • Routing and logistics-management software.
  • Returns, exceptions, post-purchase operations and customer impact.

What this shows

Complex-industry content becomes easier to read when the writer understands the system behind it.

The value here was not simply knowing logistics vocabulary. It was learning the workflow well enough to identify the reader’s actual job, structure the explanation around it, and connect the topic to software only when that connection made sense.

That is the same approach I use for other complex B2B categories: understand the operation first, simplify the explanation second, and optimise the page around the intent without flattening the subject.
