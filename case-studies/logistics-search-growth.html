Case study 03 · FarEye / Logistics SaaS

Helping logistics content rank for the questions buyers were already searching for.

The work covered operational education and software evaluation across last-mile delivery, route planning, tracking, direct-store delivery, returns, and logistics systems.

The challenge

Logistics topics get specific quickly.

A reader searching for direct-store delivery, last-mile tracking, routing software, or delivery exceptions is usually trying to solve a real operational problem. The content therefore had to do more than define terms. It had to explain the workflow clearly enough to be useful to people working in supply chain and delivery operations.

What I covered

The work moved from education into software evaluation.

Some pages answered early questions about processes and terminology. Others helped readers compare software or understand how technology fits into the workflow. That gave the content mix coverage across more than one point in the buyer journey.

  • Direct-store delivery and distribution processes.
  • Last-mile carrier tracking and delivery visibility.
  • Routing and logistics-management software.
  • Returns, delivery exceptions, and post-purchase operations.

How I approached it

Understand the operation before writing about the software.

I researched how the workflow actually works, what the reader is trying to fix, which terms people use when they search, and where software becomes relevant. That helped keep the content useful instead of turning every article into a product pitch.

What this shows

Niche content works better when you learn the workflow first.

For me, technical depth is useful only if it helps the reader. This work shows how I handle unfamiliar or operational industries: understand the process, find the real search question, and explain it in a way that still connects back to the product when appropriate.
