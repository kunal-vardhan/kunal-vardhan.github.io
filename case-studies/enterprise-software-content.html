Making complex enterprise-software topics useful to buyers and searchable at the same time.

Context

Enterprise-software buyers need clarity before they need persuasion.

Topics such as implementation cost, digital adoption, software utilization, and ROI sit between technical detail and business decision-making. The content has to explain the category accurately while helping buyers understand cost, risk, value, and the next question they should ask.

Strategic direction

Use buyer education to connect category knowledge with commercial intent.

The work covered both category education and high-intent topics. That means a reader can enter through a broad problem such as digital adoption or a more commercial question such as implementation cost, while the content still connects back to product relevance and business outcomes.

What the content needed to do

  • Translate complex enterprise workflows into understandable language.
  • Handle cost, implementation, adoption, and ROI without losing technical accuracy.
  • Use credible research and real enterprise examples where relevant.
  • Align content structure with buyer questions and search intent.

Execution

Research-heavy content with a strong decision-making structure.

The ServiceNow implementation-cost piece breaks the topic beyond licensing into training, consultants, scope, staffing, customization, and post-launch support. The digital-adoption work connects software utilization to productivity, training, process efficiency, and ROI.

That kind of content requires more than a surface-level definition. It needs a clear research trail, a buyer-oriented structure, and enough product context to make the page commercially useful without turning it into a sales pitch.

What this shows

Enterprise content has to work for search, the buyer, and the product at the same time.

This work reflects the type of content I want to do more of: complex B2B topics where strategy, research, search intent, buyer education, and commercial relevance all have to fit together.
