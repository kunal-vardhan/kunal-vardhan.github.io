Explaining complex enterprise software without making the reader work for it.

The content sat between technical detail and buying decisions, covering implementation cost, digital adoption, ROI, software usage, and enterprise workflows.

The challenge

Enterprise buyers need clarity before they need persuasion.

Questions about implementation cost, adoption, utilization, and ROI often involve technical detail, operational risk, and budget at the same time. The content had to be accurate enough to build trust while still being easy to follow for a business reader.

What the content needed to do

Answer the buyer’s actual question, then connect it to the product.

The work included broad category education and higher-intent topics. A reader might enter through a question about digital adoption or a direct cost query, but the page still needed to explain the topic before introducing product relevance.

  • Break complicated implementation and adoption topics into understandable parts.
  • Cover cost, risk, training, support, ROI, and operational impact where relevant.
  • Use credible research and enterprise examples instead of vague claims.
  • Keep the page commercially useful without turning it into a sales pitch.

How I approached it

Research first, structure second, product context where it belongs.

The ServiceNow implementation-cost piece, for example, goes beyond licensing into training, consultants, project scope, staffing, customization, and post-launch support. The digital-adoption work connects software usage to productivity, training, process efficiency, and ROI.

That structure helps a reader understand the decision before they are asked to consider a solution.

What this shows

Good enterprise content has to satisfy three people at once.

The reader needs a clear answer, search needs enough context to understand the page, and the business needs the content to connect naturally to the product. This is the kind of B2B work I enjoy most.
