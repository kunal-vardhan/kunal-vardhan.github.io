Content strategy & planning

Please don't give me a content calendar yet.

Before I plan topics, I need enough context about the product, the people buying it, the questions they ask, what already exists, what competitors cover, and what the business is trying to achieve. The calendar comes after that.

No Time-Turner. I actually have to decide what comes first.

What I look at first

Six inputs. One plan. The lines matter.

This is an illustrative view of how I plan. The actual mix changes with the product, market, team, and goals.

Product

What it does, who it is for, how it is positioned, and where the complexity sits.

Buyer

Problems, questions, objections, buying stages, and the language people use.

Search demand

SERPs, competing pages, intent, keyword gaps, and topics already gaining traction.

Content
plan

Existing content

What is useful, what overlaps, what is outdated, and what deserves a refresh instead of a replacement.

Sales + SMEs

Questions prospects ask, product details that need accuracy, and objections content can help answer.

Business priority

Which products, markets, use cases, or buyer decisions matter most right now.

Turning that into a plan

A good topic can still be the wrong asset.

The subject alone does not tell me whether the reader needs an educational guide, comparison, cost page, landing page, troubleshooting piece, or sales asset.

Quarterly roadmap

The roadmap decides what gets attention. The calendar decides when.

I use the roadmap to balance new coverage, commercial opportunities, refreshes, internal linking, distribution, and the amount of work the team can realistically execute.
