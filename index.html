My content strategy process

I decide what should exist before I decide what should be written.

The goal is not to fill a calendar. I look at the product, audience, buyer journey, search demand, competitors, existing content, and business priorities first, then build the roadmap around the gaps that matter.

01

Understand the business

Product, ICP, positioning, sales motion, priorities, and the decisions buyers need help making.

02

Map the buyer journey

What people need to understand at TOFU, solve or compare at MOFU, and evaluate at BOFU.

03

Find the gaps

Search intent, SERPs, competitors, keyword gaps, existing pages, sales questions, and missing coverage.

04

Build the topic system

Pillars, supporting clusters, internal-link paths, content types, and product connections.

05

Prioritize the roadmap

Business value, buyer need, ranking opportunity, funnel coverage, resources, and what should be refreshed instead of recreated.

06

Measure and improve

GA4, Search Console, product-path signals, refresh opportunities, distribution performance, and next-cycle decisions.

How I think about content

A few rules I keep coming back to.

These are the checks I use when deciding whether a content idea is worth the time.

01

The plan should start with the product and buyer, not the keyword tool.

Search data is useful after the business context is clear. Otherwise a roadmap can rank for topics that do very little for the company.

02

Technical content usually fails before the writing starts.

If the workflow, product, or buyer problem is misunderstood, better prose cannot rescue the page.

03

Publishing is not the finish line.

Content needs internal links, distribution, measurement, refreshes, and sometimes consolidation. The next roadmap should learn from what happened after publish.
