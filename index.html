My content strategy process

I decide what should exist before I decide what should be written.

The goal is not to fill a calendar. I look at the product, audience, search demand, buyer journey, competitors, and existing content first, then build the roadmap around the gaps that matter.

01

Understand the business

Product, ICP, positioning, sales motion, priorities, and the questions buyers need answered before they move.

02

Find the gaps

Search intent, SERPs, competitors, keyword gaps, existing pages, and the parts of the buyer journey the site is not covering well.

03

Build the topic structure

Pillar pages, supporting clusters, TOFU/MOFU/BOFU coverage, and internal-link paths so related pages strengthen each other.

04

Prioritize the roadmap

Search demand matters, but so do business value, ranking opportunity, funnel stage, and how closely a topic connects to the product.

05

Turn it into briefs and content

Detailed briefs, SME input where useful, research, structure, writing, editing, and enough product context to make the page useful.

06

Measure and improve

GA4 and Search Console show what is working, what is slipping, what needs a refresh, and what should influence the next cycle.

How I think about content

A few rules I keep coming back to.

These are not slogans. They are the checks I use when I’m deciding whether a content idea is worth the time.

01

A keyword is not a strategy.

If a topic has search volume but no clear audience, product, or business reason behind it, ranking for it can still be a waste.

02

Traffic only matters when it is relevant.

I care about visibility, but I care more about whether the page answers a real buyer question and moves someone closer to understanding the product.

03

Publishing is not the finish line.

Pages need internal links, measurement, refreshes, and sometimes repurposing. A content program should learn from what happens after publish.
